# Frontend layout: `src/utils/popup/`

- `src/utils/popup/popupFit.ts` — where a map popup stands once open (TJ, 2026-09-29): given the marker's screen point, the card's size, the part of the map a card can be seen in (above the results sheet) and what stands over it (every `data-map-overlay` surface: the map's rows, the legend card and the forecast player's bar, plus the tutorial's sheet card), the side to build the card on and how far the map must move so the card shows the most of itself, title first where it cannot fit whole. It also decides when a card is too tall for that area: `capHeight` is the tallest card the fit can place whole, or null when the card already stands whole or when the room left is under `MIN_CAPPED_PX` and a scrolled card would be a keyhole (TJ, 2026-10-08) Record: [0123](../../../../docs/decisions/0123-popup-grid-and-scroll-cap.md). Pure and pinned by `popupFit.test.ts`; `map/resultsLayer.ts` converts the pixels and applies it to every popup, clicked or flown to. Separate from `popupChrome.ts`, which is what a card is made of, not where it goes.
- `src/utils/popup/popupChrome.ts` — the chrome every map popup is built from: `popupCard`, the one card every popup that describes a thing wears (#738), its header band holding the title, its link out and the facts line, then the notices, the body and the action, each left out when empty; the `label: value` rows; `factsLine` and `bodyLines`, an overlay's kind-and-figure line (cut with an ellipsis, since a route or a satellite is third-party text of any length) and its plain body lines; and the marker popup's measurements as a grid (`metricGrid`, a `<table>` with row and column headers, each column a band in `GRID_BAND_COLOR` behind a gutter of the card's own surface, a hairline in `GRID_LINE_COLOR` between lines, and the heads centred and semibold over a rule) with `capPopupBody`, which caps a too-tall card's body so it scrolls under the title, `factsRow`, the coordinates on one line and the type, elevation and slope on the next, parted by the app's middle dot, which a screen reader skips, the pair linked to CalTopo through `caltopoUrl.ts` and the elevation plain (#712; two lines since #832, record [0253](../../../../docs/decisions/0253-slope-on-every-card-and-the-right-click-card.md)), `factValue`, a number in the values' face, and `colorSwatch`, a square of a layer's own colour before the number it keys, edged in `--popup-swatch-edge` (the legend swatches' outline), where the yellow slope band would read about 1.1:1 as text, and `resultPopupWidth`, the marker popup's own width rule: at most 374px, or the map less 10px a side, sized from `WIDEST_GRID_PX`, the widest label beside the widest numbers measured on macOS, where every other popup keeps `popupWidth`'s four-fifths share. `compactGrid` says when a map is too narrow for that grid at full inset, and `metricGrid` then takes 2px band insets and a 6px label gap; every number in the ranked family wears the table's colour for it (`PopupTint`, the fill inline and both themes' inks as `--tint-dark`/`--tint-light`, which `map.css` picks between on `POPUP_TINT_ATTR`), the ranked number is semibold, the app's heading weight (600, not 700), `GRID_RANKED_COLOR` marks a ranked number only where its scale gives no colour, and every link in a card (`popupLink`) is in the colour of the text around it, as the table's are: a grid number in the card's ink, the coordinates in the facts line's grey. It carries `data-popup-link`, which `map.css` underlines on hover and focus and faintly at rest on touch, as the table's `LINK_QUIET` does, and the title's link-out glyph (`data-popup-glyph`) rests in the label grey and takes the accent on hover (record [0212](../../../../docs/decisions/0212-links-underline-on-hover-and-touch.md)). `popupButton` is the panel's `BUTTON_ACCENT` worn as its class list, one look for every card's one action, add or remove. Every colour it names is a `var(--popup-*)` that `map.css` declares once as the app's own `--ui-*` token for the part it is (#740, #786), so a card is the panel's surface, inks and rules in either theme; `styles.test.ts` fails a `--popup-*` value that is not a token and measures every pair in both, and `popupParity.spec.ts` reads the rendered card and button against the panel's. Record: [0163](../../../../docs/decisions/0163-popups-follow-the-theme.md) Record: [0215](../../../../docs/decisions/0215-popup-is-the-panel.md) `popupCard` draws that card for a ranked result, a clicked feature (0139), a fire, a closure and a smoke plume (0161): the name and its facts on the header band, the grid's own column colour, with a faint shadow in place of the rule (TJ chose this neutral band, option H of eight, over the sky-100 one first shipped to the preview), keeping `map.css`'s `--popup-close-lane` clear (2rem, or 3.375rem on a touch screen, whose 44px close button reaches the facts line), and the grid 10px from both edges. Every popup's title keeps one line and ends in an ellipsis, as the table's names do (TJ, 2026-10-08) Record: [0123](../../../../docs/decisions/0123-popup-grid-and-scroll-cap.md). A string module rather than a component because the result is handed to MapLibre's `setHTML`. The design system still reaches it: Tailwind emits a class for any name it finds in the source, wherever the string goes, and a popup is in the same document as the panel, which is how its button is `BUTTON_ACCENT` (record 0215). The belief that it could not is what gave the popup a palette of its own, which drifted. The label/value split is COLOUR rather than weight, and that is a measurement rather than a taste: under the generic `sans-serif` Chrome on macOS resolves exactly two faces, and under the app's stack the weights still read alike in pairs, so a weight step does not hold across faces. The card sets no face of its own: it inherits the app's through `map.css`, so a popup and the panel are one face (record [0217](../../../../docs/decisions/0217-popup-in-the-app-face.md)). The popup's one `<strong>` therefore stays on the title. `linkIcon` takes the title's link as a `PopupCardLink`, the url with the name a screen reader hears for it (`aria-label`, since the glyph is `aria-hidden`: `destinationLinkLabel` for a destination, `FIRE_LINK_LABEL` and `CLOSURE_LINK_LABEL` for the overlays, #738), so no card links out unnamed. `popupLink` and `linkIcon` escape their url themselves, so a caller hands them the raw one: an href is the one value in this markup inside an attribute, where a quote mark ends it and the rest becomes markup, and a pin restored from a share link carried its OSM id there unescaped until #621. Since #365 it also owns the popup's type ramp (`POPUP_TITLE_SIZE`, `POPUP_BODY_SIZE`, `POPUP_FINE_SIZE`, `POPUP_LEADING`, all four unexported, so only `popupChrome.ts` sets them), the names of the rule, glyph, fine-print and warning colours, `fineprint` and `popupButton` (the POI card's add and remove, a ranked card's remove, the ring's remove-point), and the overlay cards in `wildfires.ts`, `smoke.ts` and `closures.ts` compose it; the linter's `style-popup-card` check fails a `*PopupHtml` builder that never calls `popupCard` and a `<strong>`, `<br>` or `<hr>` written anywhere else, which is what kept the three overlays on a ruled shell of their own for a release after the destination card moved on Record: [0161](../../../../docs/decisions/0161-every-popup-is-the-card.md); the linter's `value-inline-style` check fails a `font-size:`, `padding:`, `border-radius:` or `background:` spelled anywhere else. `FINE_COLOR` sits under AA on the dark card (3.07:1), named with its measurement; the warning is the table's own amber (`--ui-warn`), which clears AA in both themes, where it had been a popup copy, amber-500, at 2.15:1 on white (record 0215). A result card's warnings are `warningLine`s, which carry no glyph, in a section of their own between the header and the grid, ruled off by the header's edge (`popupCard`'s `warnings`), with one ⚠️ in a column of its own down the section's right side, under the close button, two warnings bulleted, and each warning one line cut with an ellipsis (the `ONE_LINE` recipe a title wears), in the short forms `firePopupText` and `closurePopupText` beside the table's hover sentences: a colour decision is the maintainer's `popupAction` is a card's one centred action in its foot (#696), which the clicked feature's `Add to analysis` and a ranked card's `Remove from analysis` both wear, through `popupCard`'s `action`. On the ranked card that foot is a section of its own, ruled off from the grid by `SECTION_EDGE` and `SECTION_PAD_Y`, the edge and padding the warnings section wears. Record: [0143](../../../../docs/decisions/0143-row-opens-its-popup.md) `bodySection` rules a second part of a body off from the first with the same edge, run through the body's sides, and `sectionHeading` names each part, for the card that carries the radar and the snow under one click (#737); that heading is the one semibold line besides the title, since a row's label and value never go bold. `updatedLine` is the italic fine print every card whose source says when it measured ends on (a fire's survey, the snow analysis, the radar frame), with `updatedAt` its date and time (through `dateOfMs`, so a stamp past Date's range leaves its line off), `monthDayTime` the yearless date and time a plume's window and a fire detection's card read (`Oct 29, 10:12 AM`), `ageAgo` the age the radar's frame reads (`now`, `40 min ago`, `1 hour ago`, `3 hours ago`, each count rounded down), where a detection's card reads its own shorter forms (`detectedAgo` in `fireActivity.ts`, #799), and `pendingValue` the bar a value still on its way stands as Record: [0165](../../../../docs/decisions/0165-radar-and-snow-answer-a-click.md) `widestName` sets a name that changes while its card is open at the width of the widest name it can take, every name in one grid cell with all but the shown one hidden from sight and from a screen reader, so the radar card stays one width through playback (#752) Record: [0168](../../../../docs/decisions/0168-radar-card-one-width.md)
- `src/utils/popup/groundFact.ts` — what a card knows of the ground under its point (`GroundState`: pending, read, or nothing to say) and how every card says it (`slopeFact`, #832): its band's swatch from 27 degrees, the whole degree under the angle so the number and the swatch agree at a band's edge, and the compass point it falls toward, or none on flat ground. One module, so the ranked, label, pending and right-click cards say a slope one way. Record: [0253](../../../../docs/decisions/0253-slope-on-every-card-and-the-right-click-card.md)
- `src/utils/popup/popupRows.ts` — a marker popup's body, derived from the COLUMNS the results table is showing rather than from a list of its own (#370). The popup used to hold six hard-coded rows, so a Current lookup read total, avg, min and max over four copies of one number, and a date-range report showed six of the sixteen values the table had. It groups by metric family, alphabetical by label whatever the ranking or the table's column order (TJ, 2026-10-08), and the label carries the unit every visible column in the group shares. Precipitation and snowfall do not share one, their window total being inches and their other three a rate, so their label names the rate and the total goes unitless into the Total column. The elevation is not a group: `popupIdentity` gives it, as `14,406 ft`, to the line under the name with the type. `popupGrid` lays the groups out as the card's grid: a row per family, Min, Max, Avg and Total as the columns in that order for every family, and no columns at all over a Current lookup (TJ, 2026-10-08). A cloud deck that held at one edge of its walk all window prints that bound once across Min, Max and Avg, because three `≥30,000` side by side outgrow the card. Each value carries `ranked`, true for the key the report ranks by, or for a one-number family that is the ranked one, since a Current lookup collapses a Max ranking onto the Avg column, and the grid marks that cell Record: [0123](../../../../docs/decisions/0123-popup-grid-and-scroll-cap.md). Each value also carries `tint`, the ranked family's colour for its number from `cellColor` in `resultsCells.ts`, the table's own, in both themes, with `pointSample` scoring precipitation and snowfall on their window scales as the table does (#740).
