# 0243. The chart draws up to four metrics, each a panel on one time axis

- Status: Accepted
- Date: 2026-10-29
- Decider: the maintainer (TJ), who chose two stacked panels, the swap, and the link parameter on 2026-10-28, and on reviewing PR #828 on 2026-10-29 the card's headers, the + and the ×, four panels in a grid with one knob, and the names `Chart metric`, `Metric 2` to `Metric 4`, `Add a panel`, `Remove {metric label}` and `Resize the panels`
- Issues and PRs: #499, #828
- Cited in code as: #499
- Guide: `components/` guide: `TimeSeriesChart.tsx`; `hooks/` guide: `useChartMetrics.ts`; `utils/chart/` guide: `chartData.ts`, `chartPointer.ts`; `STYLES.md`; `USAGE.md`

## Context

The results chart drew one metric at a time. A reader weighing rain against temperature had to flip the dropdown and hold the first picture in their head.

## Decision

- **One panel per metric, up to four.** Each metric is its own `LineChart`, all in one `syncId` group, each with its own y axis, domain and extreme line. The Now and model-end lines stand in every panel; their labels in the first only. One metric is one panel, as before.
- **Two stack, and on a phone every count stacks.** Only the bottom one prints the time axis; the top one's is hidden and zero tall, so both plot bands span the same pixels. `ResizeGrip` moves height between the two it parts, each held to a floor of its margins, its axis strip and a 40px band (`MIN_PANEL_BAND_PX`), the room two 11px tick labels need. A double press evens them.
- **Three and four stand in a grid at a desk.** Three are a row of two over one full-width panel; four are two rows of two. Every grid panel draws its own x and y axes, since a column cannot share another's.
- **The gutters size the grid.** The vertical gutter drags the column split, the horizontal one the row split, and a 16px square where they cross drags both; with three panels the vertical gutter stops at the top row and the crossing stands at the T. Each gutter is a 1px line in the `edge` token, which clears the 3:1 a UI boundary owes on the panel (3.07:1 dark, 3.97:1 light) where `rule` did not, that turns accent on hover and while dragged. A desk's stacked panels wear the same line between them (TJ, 2026-10-30), so every desk layout's bars are one quiet line; a phone's stack keeps the raised bar a finger can find. Each bar carries a small three-dot grip at its middle and each crossing a dot, flat on the panel's fill with an `edge` rim, always shown (TJ, 2026-10-30, option B of three mockups, over a line that wakes only on hover and a grip shown only on hover): a bare line was hard for a new reader to find, and the flat grip still reads a level below the sheet's raised grip above it. Every move sets the splits, each held to [0, 1] with no floor, so dragged into a corner one panel takes the area. A panel narrower or shorter than its floor (`panelWidthFloorPx`, `panelFloorPx`) is not drawn; its gutter and its picker stay. The width floor holds two of the widest x axis ticks on one line each (`MIN_PANEL_BAND_WIDTH_PX`, 2 × 74px + 8px), because at the height's 40px a squeezed panel printed one tick wrapped onto two lines. A double press on a gutter evens its axis, and on the crossing both. A drag runs on the compositor (TJ 2026-10-30, replacing the stretch and settle of 2026-10-29): the area, the boxes and the handles are measured once when it begins, and each frame moves the boxes and handles with `translate` and stretches every panel's last drawing with `scale`, from arithmetic over that measure, writing no layout and reading none. Every moved element wears `will-change: transform` for the drag only. A 150 ms rest commits the split and the panels redraw at their real size; a release commits it and hands the panels back one a frame, the one under the pointer first, so no one task redraws them all (`useStretchDrag`). The drag reads `pointerrawupdate` where the browser sends it. The splits are session state. The + and the × are squares as tall as the picker beside them (22px at a desk, 32px on touch).
- **A phone wears the desk's row and draws all four.** A phone shipped first with two panels at most, each picker the plot's title (record 0149), and a readout that rose out of the chart into the sheet (record 0154). On review on 2026-10-30 TJ asked for the desk's picker row on a phone, with the same + and move, and up to four panels. Four stack in one column on one time axis, a grip between each pair, because half of a 2×2 grid at a phone's width is narrower than a panel's width floor (`MIN_PANEL_BAND_WIDTH_PX`). The card stays inside the chart's area on a phone as at a desk: the readout that rose into the sheet clipped under the sheet's top once the card grew a header band. Each picker then stands over its own panel's top left (`CHART_PICKER.bar`, the + after the first), because four in one row wrapped onto a second line at a phone's width (TJ, 2026-10-30, over scrolling the row sideways and truncating the labels); the plot is inset under it (`PHONE_PLOT_MARGIN`), the grip moves a panel up or down the stack with Up and Down as well as Left and Right, and a drag rings the picker it lands on (`CHART_PICKER.target`). Supersedes 0149 and 0154.
- **The + and the ×.** At a desk each slot is a `MenuSelect` at `CHART_METRIC_W`, named `Chart metric` for the first and `Metric 2` to `Metric 4` after it, each but the first with an × after it and the last with a +, which adds the first metric of `CHART_METRICS` not drawn and greys at four. The × is a segment of its picker: one field outline holds the picker on the left and a 22px × (32px on touch) behind an `edge` divider on the right (`CHART_PICKER`, TJ 2026-10-29, option A). A bare × beside the picker shipped first and was replaced on review on 2026-10-29. There is no empty slot. The row has one 8px gap between every control, the + included (TJ, 2026-10-30): the + had stood 4px after the last picker while the pickers stood 16px apart.
- **The row orders the panels.** The pickers' order is the panels' reading order, left to right and top to bottom, so a reader who moves a picker moves its panel (TJ, 2026-10-30). Every picker but the first carries a grip on its field's left end, the ×'s twin: a pointer drags it along the row to the nearest slot, an accent bar showing where it lands, and Left and Right move it one place. While dragged, the picker is carried under the pointer as the table's columns are (`DRAG.ghost`, TJ 2026-10-30). The first slot is the ranking's, so nothing moves into it or out of it; making any slot movable, with or without the ranking still steering the first, was offered and declined. The grip's accessible name is `Move {metric label}`.
- **One card.** A hover shows one card, on the hovered panel, built like the marker popup (TJ 2026-10-29, mockup 1 of four): a header band in the popup's own `band` token carries the hour as its title, a body holds the rows, and a footer ruled off below holds `+N more` when rows are cut. With more than one panel the body opens on heads naming each column with its unit, one `frame` rule across every column under them, and bare numbers below; a missing value is an empty cell. Its width and its rows are budgeted on the whole chart area rather than the hovered panel, it is as wide as its content rather than shrunk to the hovered panel's box (Recharts positions it inside that panel, where a shrink-to-fit width squeezed the name column to `Moun…` in a 2×2 grid), it may cross into a neighbouring panel, and it stays inside the area: flipped to the cursor's left at the right edge and pulled up at the bottom (`cardPlacement`). One panel's card has no header, because its picker names the unit. The nearest line is read through the hovered panel's domain and dims in every panel.
- **A finger reads only while it rests** (TJ, 2026-10-30, variant F of ten mockups). On a phone the chart fills the sheet, so a tap meant to close a standing card landed on a plot and moved the hour instead. A lift now drops the reading, as Apple Stocks and most phone charts do, so there is never a card to close; the tap's click still seeks the map's playhead (#517). A ✕ on the card and a readout in the results bar were the other two shortlisted. This amends 0144, where the reading stayed after the lift.
- **A desk picks the arrangement** (TJ, 2026-10-30, option A of three mockups, over docking a dragged panel on another's edge and a segment in the results bar). A square after the + wears the current layout's shape and opens the menu card of every layout the count offers, in one order for both counts: four as 1 over 3, 2 by 2, one column, 1, 2, 1 or one row; three as 1 over 2, 2 over 1, one column or one row; two as one column or one row. Four open as 1 over 3 and three as 1 over 2 (TJ, 2026-10-30), because the first slot is the ranking's and reads best full width over the rest; below a chart about 500px wide the bottom row's panels fall under their width floor, where 2 by 2 would not. The grid keeps its shared column split and crossing; one column is the stack on one time axis; the others stand in rows with a bar between each pair of rows and between each pair of panels in a row, each row its own, and a square wherever a row bar meets a panel bar moves both, as the grid's crossing does (`chartLayout.ts`). The choice rides the link as `layout=`, written only off the count's own, and is kept while panels come and go. The button is named `Panel layout`. A phone always stacks.
- **A click pins the card** (TJ, 2026-10-30, P2 of four mockups, over pinning one card, one card holding two hours, and Space growing the card). At a desk a click on a plot moves the map player as before and pins the card at that hour: it stops following the pointer, wears the accent ring and a ✕ named `Unpin {hour}`, and its body scrolls through every row, ordered by the clicked panel's metric. Shift and a click pins a second hour beside it, so two hours read side by side; a third replaces the older, a click without Shift starts over. The card pinned or touched last is the active one and Escape lets it alone go, so a second press closes the other. A pinned card keeps the hover card's height, moves by its header inside the plotting area, sizes by a grip in its bottom-right corner, and sorts by any head, Name included, an accent arrow on the head that orders it (TJ 2026-10-30). Each pinned hour is a dashed line in the playhead's colour in every panel. The header keeps the hour alone (H1). A phone pins nothing: its reading lasts only while a finger rests.
- **Slots swap.** The first slot follows the ranking. A metric another slot holds swaps the two, whether the ranking or a picker asks (`pickMetric`).
- **The link.** `chart=precip,temp,wind,aqi`, first panel first, written only when there is more than one slot or the first is off the ranking's family. Decoding drops what it does not know, repeats and a fifth name.
- **The hover budget counts every drawn panel.** `tracksCursor` is handed lines times drawn panels.
- **A comparison stays off while any drawn panel is air quality**, for the reason it was off before: CAMS answers the same AQI whatever model ranked the field.

## Alternatives rejected

- **Two y axes on one plot.** Two scales over one set of lines reads as one scale, and a hover could not say which axis the cursor stands against.
- **An empty second slot reading None.** It was the first build (2026-10-28) and stood as a control with nothing in it; a + says what it does.
- **One knob where the gutters cross.** Shipped first, and replaced on review on 2026-10-29: a 14px knob was hard to find and could not move one split alone.
- **The heads on the band.** Variants with the column heads on the header band under the hour, and with the more line kept in the body, were shown and declined on 2026-10-29.
- **More than four.** Each panel at a laptop's chart height would be shorter than its own axis labels can stand in.
